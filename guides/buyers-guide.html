BMW Buyer’s Guide 2025

Introduction

BMW’s 2025 lineup offers something for nearly every type of driver, from efficient EV commuters to high-performance M cars. This guide helps prospective buyers understand the lineup, narrow down options, and make informed decisions.

Body Styles

BMW divides its lineup into familiar categories: Sedans, Coupes, Gran Coupes, Convertibles, Sports Activity Vehicles (SAVs), and Sports Activity Coupes (SACs).

  • Sedans: Practical and balanced; includes the 3, 5, and 7 Series.
  • Coupes/Convertibles: Focused on sporty aesthetics; 2 and 4 Series dominate.
  • Gran Coupes: Blend coupe styling with four-door practicality.
  • SAVs/SACs: BMW’s term for SUVs, from the compact X1 to the luxurious X7 and XM.

Powertrain Choices

Buyers can choose between internal combustion, plug-in hybrid, or full electric.

Price Bands

  • Entry ($35k–$45k): 230i, X1
  • Mid ($45k–$75k): 330i, 530i, X3, X5 40i
  • Premium ($75k–$100k): M340i, i5, X5 PHEV, M cars
  • Luxury ($100k+): 7 Series, i7, XM

Technology

All modern BMWs include iDrive 8.5 with curved displays, OTA updates, and driver-assist packages. Higher trims include Highway Assistant and rear-seat entertainment.

Buyer Personas

  • Young Professional: 330i or 230i Coupe
  • Family Commuter: X3 or X5
  • Performance Driver: M2, M3, or M4
  • Eco-conscious: i4, iX, i7

FAQs

  • Are BMWs expensive to maintain? Maintenance is higher than economy brands but includes 3 years of scheduled service.
  • Which BMW EV has the longest range? The iX and i7 typically exceed 300 miles.
